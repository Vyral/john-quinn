You have probably seen the ads promising that real estate is a fast track to getting rich: buy a house, pull out the equity, buy another, and flip the whole thing in a couple of years. After almost forty years of investing in Memphis real estate, I can tell you the real version is less flashy and far more reliable. You can build genuine wealth in property. It just does not happen the way most people are sold on.

**It starts with one honest example.** I recently sold a house I had bought back in 2009 for twenty-nine thousand dollars. I put about fifteen thousand into it to make it nice, then rented it out for seventeen years. The rent began around seven hundred dollars a month and eventually reached about a thousand. That was never a windfall. After taxes, insurance, and repairs, very little was left over each month. Yet when I sold the property recently for a hundred and thirty-five thousand dollars, I walked away with roughly a hundred and twenty-five thousand after expenses.

**The money was never in the rent, and that is the part people miss.** Most new investors buy a rental believing the monthly cash flow will make them wealthy. It rarely does. You might collect eight or nine hundred dollars a month and pay out three or four hundred in taxes, insurance, and upkeep. That is not wealth, it is a modest side income for real work. The wealth arrives much later, once the property has appreciated and the loan is paid down. On that 2009 house, my tenants covered the expenses for seventeen years while the market did the rest.

{% include pullquote.html %}

**Think of it as a retirement plan, not a quick score.** Imagine you are in your late twenties or early thirties and you commit to buying just one property a year. As your income grows, you add a couple more. You do not need an expensive property each time, and in Memphis you can still find very affordable ones. Twenty years later, a portfolio of thirty properties, each netting around a hundred thousand dollars, represents three million dollars in today's money, and likely much more once two more decades of appreciation are counted. One of the very first houses I ever sold, nearly forty years ago, went for sixty thousand dollars. That same house is worth close to four hundred thousand today.

**None of this is effortless, and that honesty matters.** You will get the call at eight o'clock at night about a flooded bathroom. You will deal with a difficult tenant from time to time. And you cannot simply buy the first house you see, because there are plenty of investors competing for property right now. Finding one where the numbers genuinely work takes patience and effort.

**That said, the current Memphis market is creating real openings.** Right now there are more homes on the market than we have seen in about fifteen years, and the pace of sales has slowed more than at any point in roughly two decades, a shift the [local Memphis market reports](https://www.maar.org/) have tracked through 2026. Prices are softening, and some sellers need to move. If I were twenty years younger, I would be actively buying. When real deals appear, this is the kind of market in which you find them.

So the honest truth is this. You do not get rich in real estate overnight. You build wealth by buying sound properties, holding them, letting your tenants pay them down, and giving appreciation the years it needs to work.
